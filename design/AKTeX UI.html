On the Convergence of Gradient Descent
in Non-Convex Settings
Oussama Akhail · University Ibn Zohr · 2026
Abstract
We study the convergence properties of gradient descent in non-convex optimization settings. Under mild smoothness assumptions, we establish rates that match known lower bounds and extend prior results to stochastic regimes.
1   Introduction
Let f : ℝⁿ → ℝ be a differentiable function satisfying the L-smoothness condition. The gradient descent update rule is given by:
xk+1 = xk − αk ∇f(xk)
where αk > 0 denotes the step size at iteration k [1]. We assume the objective satisfies:
∥∇f(x) − ∇f(y)∥ ≤ L∥x − y∥, ∀x, y ∈ ℝⁿ
2   Main Results
Under the assumptions above, gradient descent with constant step size α = 1/L satisfies the following convergence bound after T iterations:
mink≤T ∥∇f(xk)∥² ≤ 2L(f(x0) − f*) / T
This result is tight in the sense that no first-order method can achieve a faster rate in the worst case over the class of L-smooth non-convex functions.
3   Proof Sketch
The key insight is the descent lemma, which follows from L-smoothness:
